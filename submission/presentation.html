The signal

What the router actually looks at

A price ladder, and named moves

Nine models, $0.20 to $10.00 per million input tokens. Low-stakes wording starts at the bottom rung; high-stakes wording starts three rungs up. Then six rules move it on what actually happened: a stakes tool really fired +3 · stuck retrying +4 · resumed from a background wait −1. Every fired rule is logged, so any decision reduces to one line.

Checked against data, not intuition

Our first stakes list included send and message. It fired on 411 of 1,000 tasks — almost all routine Slack posting, not risk. Narrowing it to genuinely irreversible actions dropped that to 56, and that fix is what took the router from worse than baseline to better than it.

Before we claim anything

The bill is $155, not $87

The starter kit prices input text only. It misses ~4,200 tokens of tool definitions shipped on every call, and it misses output, billed at up to 5× the input rate. Counted properly, the logged corpus is $155.23, not $87.42 — the kit understates its own baseline by 44%.

We also found a caching bug in it: its grouping hash credits a discount on 35,344 tokens that were never shared. Leaving that discount in reproduces their printed $87.42 exactly; removing it gives $87.52. That is how we know where the error is.

Every percentage on the next slides is a percentage of $155.23.

The one chart

Cost–quality frontier (cache-aware · 95% bootstrap CIs)

Quality is plotted as % of what Viktor gets today. Held-out: $90.29 at 98.83%, CI [98.41, 99.23]. Starter baseline $121.35 at 99.36%. Always-dearest $295.57 — 212% of the bill for +0.7% quality.

Honesty

What generalised, and what didn't

  • Held — the cost cut. 62.6% → 64.8% of the logged bill, on data the router had never seen. That claim we stand behind.
  • Didn't — the quality win. On the build split we were +0.36pp over the baseline, but the interval [−0.20, +0.93] spans zero, so it was never real. Held out it is −0.53pp, CI [−0.90, −0.13], which excludes zero. The baseline is genuinely a little better on quality, for 22 more points of the bill.
  • And the one we won't bury — on our own estimator, routing everything to the cheapest model scores higher than we do (99.65% vs 98.83%). We think that is selection bias — cheap models look good because the operator only trusted them with easy work — but we cannot prove it with this data. So we are not claiming a quality win.
